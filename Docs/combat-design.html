The constraint that shapes everything

There is no block animation

The Katana Girl rig ships with 6 attacks, 5 evasive moves, 2 hit reactions, a stun and a death. It has no guard, no parry, no shield.

This is the single most important fact in the project, and it is good news. A souls-like with no block button cannot be a shield-turtle game — it has to be built on spacing, commitment and reads, closer to Bloodborne than to Dark Souls. Every defensive decision the player makes must come out of five movement clips, which means those five clips have to carry real weight and feel meaningfully different from one another.

The second constraint follows from the first: six attack animations is a small pool. The fight cannot be made engaging through move quantity. It has to come from frame data, from a boss that reads and punishes, and from the way each unlock re-frames the moves you already had. In a one-boss game that is the right place for the fun to live anyway.

The existing School_Katana_Controller is a flat 38-state demo controller with one trigger per clip, built for the asset pack's button-viewer. It is not salvageable for gameplay. We author a new controller and leave the demo one untouched for reference.

Core loop

Fight, die, learn, retry — in under two seconds

Death is the progression mechanic, so death must be cheap. From killing blow to next attempt: one death animation, one unlock card, one input. No menus, no loading, no walk back to the fog gate.

Starting kit is two moves, not one

The premise says you begin knowing one attack. You also begin knowing how to dodge. With no player stamina and no block, evasion is the only defensive verb in the game — a player without it has no interaction beyond swinging, and the first fight becomes a cutscene. K_Attack_1 plus K_Evade is still the absolute minimum souls grammar: threaten, evade, punish. Everything else is earned.

Two dodges that feel different

With a pool this small, the distinction between Evade and Quick Shift carries a disproportionate share of the depth. Evade is the generous, forgiving roll — long invulnerability, slow recovery, gets you out of trouble but gives up your position. Quick Shift is tight, directional and less invulnerable, but recovers fast enough to keep you inside her range. Learning when to trade the safe roll for the aggressive dash is the skill curve of this game.

The shape of the table is the design. Normal-stance slashes are cheap, chainable and low-commitment — you can throw Slash 1 and still dodge her answer. The full three-hit chain does 32 damage and 52 posture but locks you for 2.1 seconds, which is long enough for her to punish if you started it at the wrong time. Special skills trade all of your safety for posture damage: Skill 3 has half a second of startup, which she will read and walk out of unless you have already earned the opening.

Because posture decays, chip damage from a safe poke-and-retreat game goes nowhere — the meter drains faster than you can fill it at that pace. The only way to break her is sustained pressure, which means aggression is the correct defense, and the player discovers that themselves rather than being told. When the meter fills she drops into K_Stun for 2.5 seconds and takes double damage: exactly long enough for one full three-slash chain, which is why the chain exists.

The player has no posture meter and no stamina. What stops them mashing attack is recovery frames and a boss that punishes whiffs.

Resolution rules

  • Record the damage source on every hit; on death, the killing move is the candidate.
  • Map it 1:1 to its player equivalent — it is a mirror match, so every boss move has one.
  • If that move is already unlocked, walk the ladder above and grant the first locked entry instead.
  • If the candidate is a Special skill and the Draw is still locked, grant the Draw instead. You cannot learn a technique you cannot take the stance for. Narratively: you learned to draw the blade.
  • Every death grants exactly one unlock until the pool is empty. A player must never walk away from a death with nothing.

Because she is static and uses her whole moveset from the start, unlock order will vary between players — someone who keeps dying to her lunge learns different things, in a different order, than someone who keeps getting caught by the three-hit chain. The ladder exists to guarantee coherence, not to override the premise.

Opponent

She never changes. You do.

Fixed behaviour, full moveset, from the first attempt to the last. The entire difficulty curve is the player's growing toolkit and the player's growing skill.

Selection by range, not by script

  • Close, under 2.5m — normal-stance slash chains, highest frequency. This is where most of the fight happens.
  • Mid, 2.5–5m — Quick Shift F to close the gap, or Skill 2 as a committed lunge that covers the distance.
  • Far, over 5m — runs in, or holds Special stance and walks, baiting you into approaching first.

The three rules that make her fair

  • Maximum three attacks, then a mandatory 0.8s neutral gap. Without a guaranteed opening, a fight with no block button reads as unfair no matter how good the player is. This is the most important line of AI code in the project.
  • Every attack telegraphs. We cannot author new animation, so tells come from elsewhere: a brief animator speed dip before the swing, a weapon trail that colours in during startup, and a distinct sound per move. The telegraph is what converts a death from unfair to instructive — and since deaths are the progression system here, they have to read as instructive.
  • She punishes whiffs, but not always. On player recovery, roughly a 30% chance to immediately punish. Always punishing is oppressive; never punishing means the player can mash. The uncertainty is what makes her feel like an opponent.

She also needs a patience timer — if the player retreats and circles without committing, she closes the distance rather than waiting. A boss you can stall is a boss you can solve boringly.

Architecture

Twelve scripts and one new controller

Move definitions are ScriptableObjects because tuning is where the fun gets made, and you will do it a hundred times. Everything else stays lean.

MoveDefinition.cs
ScriptableObject. Trigger name, speed multiplier, damage, posture damage, hitbox open/close times, cancel window, i-frame window, root-motion flag.
CombatActor.cs
Shared base for player and boss. Health, current move, state gating, hit routing, death.
PostureSystem.cs
Boss-only. Accumulation, decay timer, break into stun, damage multiplier while stunned.
PlayerLocomotion.cs
CharacterController movement, lock-on strafing, animator speed parameter.
PlayerCombat.cs
Input to move execution, chain buffering, cancel windows, i-frame application.
BossAI.cs
Range bands, weighted move selection, combo counter, mandatory gap, whiff punish, patience timer.
Hitbox.cs / Hurtbox.cs
Weapon-mounted trigger collider, enabled by animation event. One hit per swing per target.
AnimationEventRelay.cs
Bridges animation events on the model to components on the root.
HitReaction.cs
Picks K_Hit_L or K_Hit_R from the angle between hit direction and forward. Free polish.
ProgressionManager.cs
Persists unlocks, records the killing move, runs the resolution rules, survives scene reload.
LockOnCamera.cs
Replaces the demo free-fly camera entirely. Target framing, soft follow, break distance.
HitFeedback.cs
Hitstop, camera shake, impact VFX, sound. Small file, disproportionate effect.

Decide root motion on day one

Root motion on for attacks and dodges — the clips were authored with displacement and stripping it makes every swing feel weightless. Root motion off for locomotion, where scripted movement is more responsive. Drive it per-state from a flag on the move asset. Getting this wrong and discovering it in week three is the classic way this kind of project loses a week.

Risk register

What actually kills this project

Boss AI eats the schedule
MitigationHard three-day budget. Weighted random over range bands with cooldowns — no behaviour tree library, no utility AI framework. A simple opponent with good telegraphs beats a sophisticated one that arrives on day 25.
Clip timings don't match the targets
MitigationPer-move animator speed multiplier on the move asset, measured and set on day 1. Assume every number in the frame data table is wrong until you have played it.
Game feel gets cut when time runs out
MitigationIt is scheduled in week 3, ahead of tuning and HUD, deliberately. In a game this small, hitstop and sound do more for "is it fun" than any system on this page.
The demo scripts fight the new ones
MitigationLeave the asset pack's viewer scene and controller entirely alone; build gameplay in new folders. The viewer stays useful for previewing clips.
The arc is too short or too long
MitigationTen unlocks assumes roughly one death per unlock, but a struggling player may die thirty times. Tune boss damage first — it is the fastest lever on arc length.

Held back

Deliberately not in scope

  • Map design and level art — one flat arena with a boundary. Explicitly deferred.
  • Healing items — no estus. Fights stay short, retries stay cheap, and the loop stays tight. Revisit only if playtests say the fight is too punishing to learn from.
  • The Humanoid_Bot character — useful as a training dummy in week 1, not a second enemy.
  • Crouch animations — K_Crouch_* has no combat role here. Leave it.
  • Emotion clips — the seven CBG_* expressions are for cutscenes we are not making. Possible use on the victory beat, day 29 if time allows.
  • A second phase — she is static by design. If tuning shows the late arc goes slack, an HP-gated shift into Special stance is the cheapest fix available, and the animation set already supports it.
